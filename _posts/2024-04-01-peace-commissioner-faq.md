# FAQ: Everything You Need to Know About Peace Commissioners in Ireland

I was appointed as a **Peace Commissioner (*Coimisinéir Síochána*)** for County Dublin and surrounding counties by the Minister for Justice in **April 2024**. 

I frequently receive questions from members of the public about what the role actually involves, how to get documents witnessed, and how the appointment process works. Here are answers to the most common questions I receive.

---

### **What is a Peace Commissioner?**
A Peace Commissioner is an honorary legal official appointed by the Minister for Justice under [Section 88 of the Courts of Justice Act 1924](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1924/act/10/section/88/enacted/en/html#sec88). The role was created to provide communities across Ireland with local, trusted access to essential administrative legal services without needing to go before a court judge.

* **Resource:** [Citizens Information: Peace Commissioners](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/justice/civil-law/peace-commissioners/)

---

### **What does the name mean and where does it come from?**
* **The Irish Title:** *Coimisinéir Síochána* translates directly to "Commissioner of the Peace."
* **Historical Origins:** Established shortly after the creation of the Irish Free State, the position replaced older British colonial legal roles (such as Justices of the Peace). The goal was to establish local, community-based signatories for routine official paperwork.

---

### **What can a Peace Commissioner do for you?**
A Peace Commissioner is authorized to:
* **Administer Oaths & Statutory Declarations:** Verifying declarations for housing, citizenship applications, passport declarations, and state agency forms.
* **Witness Signatures:** Verifying signatures on official documents, legal contracts, and property transactions.
* **Certify True Copies:** Attesting true copies of original documents (such as passports, birth certificates, or utility bills) where required by Irish authorities.
* **Sign Garda Documents:** Signing summonses or search warrants requested by An Garda Síochána.
* **Certify Certificates:** Endorsing official forms, such as club registration certificates under the [Registration of Clubs (Ireland) Act 1904](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1904/act/9/enacted/en/html).

---

### **What can a Peace Commissioner NOT do?**
To avoid confusion, please keep these legal limits in mind:
* **No Legal Advice:** Peace Commissioners are strictly signatories and witnesses. We cannot offer legal advice or draft legal documents.
* **No Affidavits / Court Documents:** A Peace Commissioner is **not** a Commissioner for Oaths or a Solicitor. We cannot attest affidavits for court proceedings.
* **Documents for Use Outside Ireland Only:** The title of Peace Commissioner is recognized **only within Ireland**. Documents intended for international use, foreign embassies, or foreign courts generally require a **Notary Public** or **Commissioner for Oaths**.

---

### **How much do you charge for your services?**
**Nothing—it is 100% voluntary and free.**

Under Irish law, Peace Commissioners receive no salary, fee, or expenses, and we are strictly forbidden from charging for any service provided. It is entirely a voluntary community service.

* **Resource:** [Houses of the Oireachtas: Peace Commissioners Powers & Remuneration](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2024-01-24/158/)

---

### **Do Peace Commissioners need an official stamp?**
Under Section 88 of the Courts of Justice Act 1924, a Peace Commissioner’s signature alone is legally sufficient. However, in modern practice, many state agencies, financial institutions, and legal bodies require an official Peace Commissioner stamp alongside the signature. Active Commissioners generally use a personal official seal specifying their name and appointment title.

---

### **How does someone become a Peace Commissioner?**
1. **Nomination or Application:** An individual can apply directly or be nominated by a public representative (such as a local TD or Councillor) or a local community group.
2. **Eligibility:** Candidates must be "fit and proper persons" of good character who are established members of their local community.
3. **Garda Vetting:** Candidates undergo background checks by An Garda Síochána.
4. **Formal Appointment:** The appointment is formally granted at the discretion of the Minister for Justice.

* **Resource:** [Department of Justice: Applying or Nominating a Peace Commissioner](https://www.gov.ie/en/organisation/department-of-justice/) *(Inquiries can be submitted directly via email to `peacecommissioner@justice.ie`)*

---

### **How do I find a Peace Commissioner in my local area?**
Because the official Roll of Peace Commissioners is maintained internally by the Department of Justice, the quickest ways to find one are:
1. Contact your local **Garda Station**, as they maintain active contact rosters for Peace Commissioners in your sub-district.
2. Contact the **Department of Justice Peace Commissioner Unit** directly at `01 859 2323` or email `peacecommissioner@justice.ie`.

---

### **Guidelines for Booking an Appointment**
If you need a document signed or witnessed:
1. **Bring Original Documents:** Always bring your original identity documents (Passport or Driving Licence) along with high-quality printed copies if true-copy certification is required.
2. **Do Not Sign in Advance:** If a document requires a statutory declaration or witnessed signature, **do not sign it beforehand**. It must be signed in the physical presence of the Peace Commissioner.
